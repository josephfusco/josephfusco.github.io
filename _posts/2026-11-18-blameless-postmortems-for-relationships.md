When software breaks, good teams run a blameless postmortem to understand what happened so it does not happen again. John Allspaw [wrote about this at Etsy in 2012](https://www.etsy.com/codeascraft/blameless-postmortems) ([archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20261025192059/https://www.etsy.com/codeascraft/blameless-postmortems)): when people fear blame, they hide details, and everyone learns less.

The part I borrow is the time to reflect. What follows is what I have learned from my own experience, in my relationship and in past ones.

It starts with waiting. Teams do not run a postmortem during an outage. In [*The Seven Principles for Making Marriage Work*](https://www.gottman.com/product/the-seven-principles-for-making-marriage-work/) ([archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20261022064518/https://www.gottman.com/product/the-seven-principles-for-making-marriage-work/)), John Gottman and Nan Silver write that when we are flooded, heart racing, we cannot take in what the other person is saying. They suggest a break of at least 20 minutes. This part is hard, and it takes a commitment from both people.

## The questions I ask

There is nothing strict about this. These are mine, and you can do it your own way.

1. What happened?
2. What was each person seeing?
3. Where did the signals cross?
4. What made it worse?
5. What will I do differently?

## An example

Every scenario in this post is made up, and none describes a real person or event. Here are the five answers for one evening.

1. I said I would be home by 6. I got home at 7:30 and did not text.
2. I was stuck at work, thinking "I'll explain when I get there." A partner saw 90 minutes of silence and worried.
3. To me the silence meant "busy." To a partner it meant "something's wrong."
4. Stress made me lose track of time, and my phone was in my bag.
5. Next time I text as soon as plans change: "Running late, home by 7:30. Sorry."

## Criticism and complaint

Gottman calls criticism one of the Four Horsemen, the patterns that predict breakups. Criticism attacks the person: "You never listen." A complaint names a behavior: "I felt unheard when you checked your phone while I was talking."

A postmortem stays on behavior and the conditions around it. I still own my part, without shame, so I can change.

## What I have learned so far

I do not see subtext when I am stressed. It impacts my life, and I am learning to accept it and work with it.
